\begin{abstract}
This paper establishes the mathematical framework of the Excited-State Cosmology framework, modeling the observable universe as the internal manifold of a singular cosmological fermion. We derive phenomenological parameters of the expanding universe directly from foundational quantum electrodynamics and general relativity. Specifically, we map the inflationary epoch to a $1s \rightarrow 2p$ quantum state transition, derive the Cosmological Constant from Wigner-Weisskopf spontaneous emission decay limits, and reproduce the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy scaling via the trace of the internal reduced density matrix. By unifying these derivations under a single hyper-dimensional Hilbert space, we provide a theoretical framework for addressing the horizon problem, galactic rotation curves, and the Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen paradox.
\end{abstract}

\chapter{Philosophical Implications: The Cyclic Universe}
The transition mechanics of the Excited-State Cosmology framework naturally yield a cyclical universe. If the universe ends via spontaneous emission---collapsing the $2p$ wave function back to the $1s$ ground state---the hyper-electron remains conserved, awaiting a subsequent photon absorption to trigger a new inflationary epoch. This maps directly to a conformal cyclic cosmology. 

Historically, as noted by physicists such as Carl Sagan and Fritjof Capra \cite{Sagan1980, Capra1975}, this specific model of cosmic creation and destruction mirrors the earliest conceptualizations of a cyclical universe found in Vedic cosmology. In the Hindu framework, the continuous expansion and dissolution of reality is described as the rhythmic respiration of Maha Vishnu: exhalation generates countless expanding universes, while inhalation absorbs and destroys them. Within the hyper-electron model, this macroscopic ``respiration'' finds a rigorous mathematical analogue in the continuous absorption (Big Bang) and spontaneous emission (Cosmic Collapse) of hyper-photons, suggesting that the ultimate fate of the universe is not a final heat death, but a localized wave function collapse preceding a new genesis.
